# How anime illustrations are rendered

Rendering turns flat line art into light, weight, and form. Each step adds one kind of light or shadow and builds on the last. Read in order and follow the arrows to see what changed.

![Finished illustration](../../gallery/guides/maren/00-maren.jpg)

## Before you paint

Pick one light source first and keep it fixed for the whole picture.

![Colour plans](../../gallery/guides/maren/a-plans.jpg)

Test several colour ideas while the drawing is still simple. Viewing options small and in three values shows at once which plan draws the eye to the face and which ones blur together. Changing direction here takes minutes instead of hours.

![Value check in grey](../../gallery/guides/maren/a-values.jpg)

Value—how light or dark a colour is—does the heavy work. Colour sets mood, but value separates shapes and guides the eye. Checking in grey strips away colour so weak contrast stands out immediately.

![Three things in every colour](../../gallery/guides/maren/c-basics.jpg)

Every colour you pick is three choices: where on the hue ring, how strong, how light. Shading well means moving all three at once. A shadow that only gets darker, with hue and strength untouched, looks grey and dirty. The square and the triangle in drawing programs hold the same colours; what matters is which way you move.

![On your colour wheel](../../gallery/guides/maren/c-wheels.jpg)

Every shade is a new pick, and each pick is three moves at once: around the ring, across the square, up or down. Shadow is never the lit colour pulled straight down. Some moves are small and some clear; the captions say which, and the steps below show what each looks like on the picture.

![Mixing a colour over another](../../gallery/guides/maren/c-blend.jpg)

Painting one colour over another can replace it, darken it, lighten it or tint it, and the result depends on how light both colours are. Multiplying only darkens and keeps both colours in the mix, which is why a shadow painted that way stays related to what is under it; a grey multiplied only dulls it. Screening only lightens. Overlay and soft light turn at middle grey: overlay looks at the colour below, soft light at the colour on top; darker than middle grey they darken, lighter than it they lighten, and middle grey itself changes nothing. Adding light burns to white fast, so it suits small sparks, or a dark paint for a soft glow.

![Form as simple solids](../../gallery/guides/maren/a-form.jpg)

Line art is flat, yet light strikes solid surfaces. Picture every part as simple solids: cylinders, boxes, and cloth draped over them. Once you see which way a surface faces, shading stops being guesswork.

![Where the light falls](../../gallery/guides/maren/a-light-map.jpg)

With your single light source set, decide per form what faces the light, what turns away, and what blocks another surface. Turn that reading into simple shadow shapes before painting detail. Planning the shadow pattern first keeps lighting consistent from head to toe.

## Step by step

### 1 · Flat colours

![1 · Flat colours](../../gallery/guides/maren/02-flats.jpg)

Flat colours lock in your value groups and separate every part before light enters. Without clean, solid flats, later shading blends parts together.

- Decide what is light, mid, and dark before choosing exact colours.
- Keep the head and face easy to spot against what surrounds them.
- Fill every shape cleanly up to the lines.

![Choosing flat colours](../../gallery/guides/maren/f-palette.jpg)

![How much: flats](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-02-flats.jpg)

**Colour:** decide how light each part is before which colour it is. Keep a few large areas quiet, not too saturated, and let small things carry the strong colour. Pick one main family and one opposite accent. Avoid pure black and pure white: even the darkest and lightest parts keep a little colour.

### 2 · Colour shifts

![2 · Colour shifts](../../gallery/guides/maren/03-colour.jpg)

Large flat shapes can feel stiff, like stickers. A gentle colour shift across a big area adds life without disturbing your values.

- Shift colour slowly across large shapes like hair or wide cloth.
- Keep lightness almost unchanged so big shapes stay clear.
- Keep it subtle; if you spot the shift first, pull it back.

![How much: colour](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-03-colour.jpg)

**Colour:** a gentle slide of hue across the shape, warmer toward the light and cooler away from it, without making it lighter or darker.

### 3 · Main shadow

![3 · Main shadow](../../gallery/guides/maren/04-shadow.jpg)

This is the most important step. Big, simple shadows on every side turned away from the light give flat lines immediate solidity. Without them, later polish floats on a flat drawing.

- Use a darker, cooler version of the base colour, never grey or black.
- Give each shape one clean side and one pointed end; place edges on a drawn line or clearly across the form, never just beside a line.
- Few large shapes beat many small ones, and simple beats strictly correct.

![How much: shadow](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-04-shadow.jpg)

**Colour:** darker, usually a little more saturated (never less), and turned away from the colour of the light: toward blue for most things, toward red for skin. Grey shadows kill the colour; very dark ones turn the picture heavy.

Common mistake: hugging every outline with thin shadows instead of grouping planes into big shapes.

![Colour on the jacket](../../gallery/guides/maren/p-jacket.jpg)

The same picks, where they sit on the jacket. That small turn of hue toward blue is what keeps a shadow looking like colour instead of dirt.

### 4 · Cast shadows

![4 · Cast shadows](../../gallery/guides/maren/05-cast.jpg)

Cast shadows tell distance. Anything standing in front of a surface throws its own shape onto what sits behind it. Without them, overlapping parts look pasted flat.

- Echo the blocking shape, shifted away from the light.
- Keep edges sharp and bold, even if a shadow looks like a blot.

**Colour:** the same colour as the main shadow on that surface; a cast is a shadow with a different shape, not a darker one.

Common mistake: softening cast shadows until overlaps lose depth.

### 5 · Half tone

![5 · Half tone](../../gallery/guides/maren/06-halftone.jpg)

Lit and shadowed shapes alone make curved forms look like cut paper. A third, light step where a form starts to turn makes it feel round.

- Keep the band narrow so it does not eat the lit side.
- Use it on curving forms and skip sharp corners.

![How much: halftone](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-06-halftone.jpg)

**Colour:** in between the lit colour and the shadow, closer to the lit side. If it reads as its own shape, it is too strong.

### 6 · Shadow edge

![6 · Shadow edge](../../gallery/guides/maren/07-edge.jpg)

On skin, light passes slightly through the surface at the turn and leaves a thin band of richer, warmer colour. Without it, skin shadows feel chalky.

- Keep the band thin along the exact light-to-shadow border.
- Make it warmer and richer than the shadow beside it.

![How much: edge](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-07-edge.jpg)

**Colour:** the most saturated colour on the skin, about as dark as the shadow, and redder. Keep it thin.

Common mistake: spreading it too wide so skin looks sunburnt.

### 7 · Deep shadow

![7 · Deep shadow](../../gallery/guides/maren/08-deep.jpg)

Open shadows still catch stray light. Only tight pockets where no light gets in—an armpit, inside folds, or tucked behind—drop to the darkest tone, giving overlaps weight.

- Keep these shapes small and dark.
- Use them sparingly; never outline every main shadow.

![How much: deep](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-08-deep.jpg)

**Colour:** continue the shadow's move: darker again, more saturated again, turned a little further. Small and few.

![Colour on khaki](../../gallery/guides/maren/p-trousers.jpg)

A dull colour has the most room to move. Khaki in light becomes rose in shadow and violet where no light gets in: the deeper the shadow, the further it travels from the lit colour.

### 8 · Shadow lift

![8 · Shadow lift](../../gallery/guides/maren/09-lift.jpg)

Big shadows, especially on dark cloth, can turn into dead holes. Lightening and cooling the shadow far from the light keeps form visible in the darks.

- Lift gently far from the light so dark cloth stays dark.
- Shift it cooler so it stays behind the lit side.

![How much: lift](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-09-lift.jpg)

**Colour:** only slightly lighter and cooler than the shadow it sits in. If the black starts to look grey or blue, it is too much.

### 9 · Light

![9 · Light](../../gallery/guides/maren/10-light.jpg)

Bright shapes on planes facing the light add clarity and pull attention upward. Keeping them selective shows which surfaces catch light directly.

- Paint one highlight block per hair lock.
- Skip white shiny spots on skin and matte cloth.

![How much: light](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-10-light.jpg)

**Colour:** lighter and less saturated, turned toward the colour of the light; on dark cloth, a soft pale sheen. Keep it a pale tint on skin, not a white spot: bright white spots read as oil.

![Colour in the hair](../../gallery/guides/maren/p-hair.jpg)

Light moves the opposite way to shadow: toward the colour of the light, here yellow, and paler. Lights and shadows pulling in two directions is what makes a surface feel lit by something.

### 10 · Reflected light

![10 · Reflected light](../../gallery/guides/maren/11-bounce.jpg)

Light bounces from the surroundings onto far shadowed edges with air behind them. That rim rounds out dark forms and separates them from the background.

- Shape it as a crisp band pointed at both ends along far edges.
- Colour it with what surrounds the figure.
- Keep it always darker than the light, or the light reverses.

![How much: bounce](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-11-bounce.jpg)

**Colour:** take the colour of what is around, usually cooler. Lighter than the shadow it sits in, clearly darker than the light. If it catches the eye before the light does, it is too bright.

![Colour on a black top](../../gallery/guides/maren/p-top.jpg)

On a near-black colour there is little room to go darker, so the shadow gains strength instead, and the reflected light is a clear step up in a colour from the surroundings. That is how black cloth still shows its form.

### 11 · Materials

![11 · Materials](../../gallery/guides/maren/12-metal.jpg)

Shading every surface the same turns metal, plastic, and cloth into clay. Distinct light behaviour makes each material believable.

- Metal puts its darkest tone right beside its brightest, plus a sharp glint.
- Plastic takes a broad, soft shine.
- Cloth stays matte with no sharp shine.

**Colour:** metal uses almost the whole range from near black to near white in a small space; its own colour stays in the darks, the colour of the light goes in the glint.

### 12 · Warm skin

![12 · Warm skin](../../gallery/guides/maren/13-skin.jpg)

Cool shadows suit clothing, but skin looks lifeless if its shade leans grey. Warming skin shade and adding a touch of blush brings back life.

- Keep skin shade warm, never grey.
- Add a soft touch of blush where skin feels thin, like cheeks.

![How much: skin](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-13-skin.jpg)

**Colour:** skin shade goes redder and more saturated, never grey. Blush is soft and well below the shadow edge in strength.

![Colour on the skin](../../gallery/guides/maren/p-skin.jpg)

Skin breaks the cool-shadow habit. Light passes into it and comes back red, so its shade turns toward red and rose and gets clearly stronger, most of all right at the edge of the shadow.

### 13 · Eyes and face

![13 · Eyes and face](../../gallery/guides/maren/14-face.jpg)

Viewers look at the eyes first. Heavy shading across the face breaks a clean anime expression, so keep the face quiet and put contrast in the eyes.

- Keep the face almost flat.
- Paint between the eye and mouth lines, never over them.
- Shade the iris dark at the top and brighter at the bottom with lights; paint each brow as one solid shape.

**Colour:** the iris is the strongest colour on the face, darker at the top and lighter below. Eye whites a touch darker than the lit skin, never pure white. Brows a darker version of the hair colour.

### 14 · Line colour

![14 · Line colour](../../gallery/guides/maren/15-linecolour.jpg)

Dark lines everywhere look harsh against bright skin or hair. Tinting inner lines lets detail sit naturally in the paint while the outline stays strong.

- Colour inner lines with a darker version of the colour they sit on.
- Keep the outline and lines between overlapping parts dark.

![How much: linecolour](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-15-linecolour.jpg)

**Colour:** about as dark as the deepest shadow of the surface, in its own colour family.

Common mistake: lightening every line equally, which washes out the drawing.

### 15 · Finish

![15 · Finish](../../gallery/guides/maren/16-finish.jpg)

Parts painted one by one can feel slightly separate. A light final pass pulls every colour and shadow into one shared light.

- Add a subtle warm tint toward the light and a cool tint away from it.
- Make parts further back slightly paler and bluer.
- Add fine grain to tie the surface together.

![How much: finish](../../gallery/guides/maren/h-16-finish.jpg)

**Colour:** a faint warm tint toward the light and a faint cool one away from it. The picture should look the same, only more together.

Common mistake: letting effects stand out; if you notice them first, pull back.

## Check your work

- **Check in grey.** Make sure the face reads first and big shapes stay apart.
- **Squint or shrink to stamp size.** Main light and shadow shapes should still read clearly.
- **Zoom in.** Clean up rough edges and stray gaps up close.
- **Fresh eyes.** Step away, then compare beside work you admire.

## Practice

- **Five-shape shadows.** Shade a figure or jacket with one shadow colour and five shapes only.
- **Cast shadows only.** Over flat colours, paint only the sharp shadows thrown by overlapping parts.
- **Three materials.** Paint one cylinder as matte cloth, smooth plastic, and metal under the same light.
- **Four colour plans.** Try four colour schemes on one drawing, check them small in three values, and pick the clearest.
